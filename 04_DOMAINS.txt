1. THE GAP TABLE IS LIVE DATA — GENERATE IT, DO NOT READ IT HERE
=======================================================================

THERE ARE TWO DOMAIN TALLIES AND THEY COUNT DIFFERENT THINGS. Say which
one you mean, every time:
  THE CORPUS TALLY      what the corpus actually holds. Read from
                        `domain = "..."` in each task.toml. This is the
                        one the generators below print.
  THE OPEN-PR TALLY     what is in flight and not yet on main. Read from
                        the GitHub `dN:` label on the pull request, by
                        tools/first_thousand.py, which folds it in beside
                        delivered/need/selected per domain.
A task.toml change moves the first. A label change moves the second.
Neither moves the other. Section 3 is about the second.

[RULE] Before acting on a domain gap, GENERATE the current numbers:

    uv run python tools/distribution_report.py

That reads policy/data/target-distribution.yaml — the 1000-task target
for the frontend-family and backend axes, the product domains, the named
stacks, the capability floors, the difficulty contract. `--check`
validates the file against itself.

For the first-delivery view:
    python3 tools/first_thousand.py report

Both are advisory. [RULE] Adoption happens through rebuild targets, never
through relabeling.

WHY THIS FILE NO LONGER PRINTS THE TABLE: a stale gap table has already
caused a wrong call here — D7 read as heavily oversupplied in an older
table and was nearly full in the current one, and the sign of the
decision flipped. A table pasted into a reference sheet is stale the
moment a task moves. The generator is three seconds.

[SNAPSHOT — for SHAPE ONLY, do not act on these numbers]
The last published shape had EVERY listed domain UNDER target, with the
largest shortfalls at D1 Marketing, D4 Content & Publishing and D2
E-commerce, and D7 Data & Analytics essentially full. The consequence of
that shape — which may or may not still hold — is that moving a task OFF
a nearly-full domain buys almost nothing; the value is moving tasks OUT
of the domains that are not short at ALL and INTO the short ones.
CHECK WHICH DOMAINS ARE SHORT TODAY BEFORE APPLYING THAT REASONING.

Target shares from the client doc, for context (these are the CONTRACT,
so they move rarely):
   D1 12% · D2 12% · D3 11% · D4 11% · D5 9% · D6 7% · D7 11% ·
   D8 8% · D9 9% · D10 5% · D12 5%
   with a floor of at least 50 tasks in every category.


=======================================================================
2. THE DEFINITIONS — the CORE ACTION decides, not the subject
=======================================================================

D1 MARKETING & LANDING PAGES
   Brand presentation, lead capture, marketing content management —
   landing pages, portfolios, event pages, agency sites — with a backend
   CMS, a lead pipeline and admin auth.
   CORE ACTION: converting a VISITOR INTO A LEAD or donor.
   NOT selling products (-> D2). NOT an operational tool (-> D3).

D2 E-COMMERCE & MARKETPLACE
   Browse a catalog, add to cart, complete a purchase — with server-side
   inventory, order persistence and authenticated buyer/seller roles.
   DEFINING PATTERN: catalog -> cart -> checkout -> order lifecycle.
   A cart is not strictly required; the ordering lifecycle and
   server-side inventory are. A returns/RMA back office and a
   stock-allocation console both file here in practice.

D3 SAAS & BUSINESS TOOLS
   Multi-user internal operational software: project management, CRM, HR
   portals, form builders, booking systems, invoicing. RBAC and
   persistent state. The app is the tool a team uses daily.
   NOT a storefront (-> D2), NOT a publication (-> D4).

D4 CONTENT & PUBLISHING
   Authoring, editing, organizing and reading structured written content
   — blogs, news, docs, wikis, CMS, magazines, recipes — with
   author/editor roles, an editorial workflow (draft -> review ->
   publish) and reader engagement (comments, search, tags).
   NOT courses with grading (-> D6).

D5 SOCIAL & COMMUNICATION
   User-to-user social interaction: posting, commenting, voting,
   following, messaging, community discussion, with authenticated
   profiles and a server-side social graph.
   CORE VALUE: the social graph and member-generated conversation.
   NOT commerce (-> D2), NOT staff-authored publication (-> D4).
   >>> THE MOST COMMONLY MISFILED DOMAIN. A staff broadcast tool with an
   approval gate and no member-to-member interaction is D4, not D5. If
   there is no social graph, it is not D5. <<<

D6 EDUCATION & LEARNING
   Structured learning content with student assessment and progress
   tracking; instructor/student roles and server-side grading. The
   defining feature is a teacher-student relationship with scored
   evaluation.

D7 DATA & ANALYTICS
   Data ingestion, aggregation, visualization and monitoring —
   dashboards, chart builders, KPI trackers, reporting. Distinguished
   from D3 by the ABSENCE of workflow/task management: the app shows
   data, it does not manage operations.

D8 ENTERTAINMENT & MEDIA
   Streaming, curating and consuming multimedia (audio, video, film,
   photography) with a server-side media catalog and persistent user
   libraries — playlists, watchlists, playback progress.
   NOT purchasing goods (-> D2), NOT editorial text (-> D4).

D9 FINANCE & PRODUCTIVITY
   Personal financial management (budgeting, expenses, portfolios,
   calculators) and individual productivity (to-dos, notes, Pomodoro).
   The user manages their OWN money or tasks — not a team's projects
   (-> D3), not a catalog (-> D2).

D10 HEALTH, FITNESS & LIFESTYLE
   Physical health, body metrics, fitness routines, nutrition, wellness,
   clinical care, with server-side health computations (1RM, calories,
   BMI) and persistent health records. NOT personal finance (-> D9).

(THERE IS NO D11. The catalog in policy/data/policy.yaml runs D1-D10 and
then D12 — verified 2026-09-08. It is a gap in the numbering, not a
domain someone forgot to document. Do not invent one.)

D12 TRAVEL & LOCAL SERVICES
   Trip planning, local discovery, booking location-based services —
   trip planners, booking portals, weather dashboards, city guides, maps
   — with server-side availability checking and persistent trip/booking
   records. NOT internal room scheduling (-> D3).

3. THE TWO ACTIONS, WHICH ARE NOT THE SAME THING
=======================================================================

### 1. LABEL SWAP — metadata. SAFE.
The OPEN-PR tally is read off the GitHub `dN:` label, so swapping it
moves that number. It does NOT move the corpus tally, which reads
task.toml — only a prose reframe (action 2) does that. Swapping a label
does not move the head, does not stale the stickies, does not re-fire
gates. A merge-ready PR stays merge-ready.

[MECHANIC] MECHANICS THAT BITE: read the PR's COMPLETE current label set
FIRST, then write it back WHOLE with the domain swapped. The update call
REPLACES the set — a partial list silently drops `hard`,
`gemini-calibrated`, `priority-1`.

### 2. PROSE REFRAME — a commit. Belongs to the PR's AUTHOR.
Rewrite the nouns in instruction.md, restamp with
`tools/stamp_stack.py <slug> --domain`.

[RULE] On a PR you did not open you never push — post the exact noun
mapping as a comment instead.

[MECHANIC] It moves the head, which re-fires the rubric judges: PRs have
gone from 0 and 2 rubric failures to 4 on the fresh run of otherwise
unchanged content. NEVER TRADE A MERGE-READY PR FOR A DOMAIN TICK
WITHOUT SAYING SO.

[RULE] A LABEL SWAP ALONE LEAVES THE TASK HALF-REFRAMED — the tally is
right, the brief still reads as the old domain. Always say that out loud.

ALSO: some PRs carry NO domain label while task.toml declares one. Those
land in first_thousand.py's `unlabelled` bucket — invisible to the
OPEN-PR tally though the task itself is labelled. Pure gap, free to fix.

[RULE] The domain mix is reached by REFRAMING a task's description, never
by rebuilding or renaming the app. A task that changes PRODUCT changes
NAME, and that is a different operation: tools/rename_task.py.


=======================================================================
4. WHAT MAKES A REFRAME HONEST
=======================================================================

[RULE] Same workflows. Same rules. Same refusals. Same figures. Only the
nouns change. ZERO lines of solution/app/ move.

If a single rule has to be RE-MOTIVATED rather than renamed, it is a
stretch — rank it lower and name the clause that is the problem.
(Real example: a harbour-swim entry desk reframes cleanly into a
limited-stock release checkout, EXCEPT the "18 or over on race day" rule,
which has to become an age-restricted-product gate. Coherent, but that
one clause is re-motivated, not renamed.)

[RULE] Some products ARE their domain and have NO honest reframe:
   - time-series gap-fill with versioned fill policies -> irreducibly D7
   - latency percentiles, SLA breach pricing, queue-drain slopes -> D7
   - telemetry ingestion quota and projected spend -> D7
   - envelope budgeting with period close and rollover -> finance, full
     stop
Forcing one of these produces a task that LIES about what it is. That is
worse than the gap. Say "no natural fit" and move on.


=======================================================================
5. A WORKED GOOD REFRAME (D12 -> D2)
=======================================================================

departure-ledger, a tour-operator seat-allotment console:

   departure                 -> product drop
   total capacity            -> total stock
   partner allotment         -> reseller allocation
   seat hold                 -> reservation line against a named order
   net fare + commission     -> wholesale price + marketplace commission

Everything survives untouched: the committed/remaining arithmetic,
`gross value = qty x net price`, half-up commission to 2dp, the
over-allocation refusal, the closed-state refusal, the cross-partner 403,
the snapshot export/restore validation.

That is what a relabel looks like. IF YOUR MAPPING TABLE CANNOT BE
WRITTEN THAT CLEANLY, YOU ARE REWRITING, NOT REFRAMING.


=======================================================================
6. A WORKED MISFILE (D5 -> D4)
=======================================================================

pulsedesk, an internal broadcast desk, was labelled D5 Social. It has no
user-to-user interaction, no social graph, no posting/commenting/voting/
following — which is the ENTIRETY of D5's definition.

What it actually models is D4's editorial workflow verbatim: an Editor
DRAFTS a broadcast, an Approver is the only role that may queue a
Critical one or mark one SENT (draft -> review -> publish with a
server-enforced approval gate); channels are publications with a cadence
ceiling and an editorial budget; the ledger's filter/search/CSV export is
content organization.

Zero behaviour change needed — the nouns were already publishing nouns.
And at the time, D4's shortfall was larger than D5's, so the correction
and the gap-fill pointed the same way.

[RULE] WHEN A DOMAIN LOOKS WRONG, CHECK IT AGAINST THE DEFINITION BEFORE
ASSUMING IT IS RIGHT. Misfiles are common and fixing one is free.
